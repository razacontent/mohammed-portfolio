The brief

Crunchyroll's TV experience worked, but it didn't know what it was. I co-shaped the rebrand from the earliest concepts and built the content systems beneath it.

The diagnosis

The surface had no Japanese cultural threading and no vocabulary that signaled the team had ever met an anime fan. Editorial picks, algorithmic recommendations, and live events all shipped as the same horizontal rail. The content function had no shared artifacts, which meant whatever shipped on TV would be a one-off.

What I built

A four-bucket module taxonomy (editorial, behavioral, algorithmic, time-bounded), a Tokyo-anchored bilingual welcome system, a metadata hierarchy with restraint rules for video-playing surfaces, and a measurement rubric so each module family had its own performance axis. Six artifacts in total, designed to travel beyond the rebrand itself.

What it did

Manga-forward direction and Hime as AI search character cleared executive review. Five internal teams adopted the artifacts as operating-system pieces for their own work. The bilingual welcome system runs across 25+ markets, and the data team can finally answer whether editorial is outperforming the recommender.

The diagnosis

A surface that could have been anything. A practice that could not travel.

Crunchyroll's TV experience worked. People watched anime on it. By every measure that mattered to fans, it didn't know what it was. By every measure that mattered to the content team, the practice underneath had nothing shared to ship from.

Four gaps the existing surface and the practice could not close

  • Speak fluent fandom. No Japanese cultural threading. No simulcast acknowledgment. No vocabulary signaling the team had ever met an anime fan.
  • Distinguish content types. Editorial picks, algorithmic recommendations, behavioral state, and live events all shipped as the same horizontal rail.
  • Carry executive defense. The proposed redesign was visually strong with no story underneath that would survive a CEO review on global scalability.
  • Travel as a practice. The content function had no shared artifacts. Whatever shipped on TV would be a one-off.

I worked all four from the first concept reviews onward. The visual rebrand and the content systems were not sequential phases. They moved together because I argued they had to.

A streaming service that licenses anime is not the same product as a home for anime fandom. The TV experience treated us like the first thing.

Reframe I brought into the first vision review / Q3 2022

The approach

Three moves. Surface fluent. Practice durable.

The vision had to clear the executive room, give engineering a system to populate, and mature the content practice into something other teams could adopt without me. Three moves, accountable to all three audiences.

Move 01 / Cultural fluency, structural not decorative

Embed Japanese cultural moments at the system level, with the welcome screen as the proof point: contextual Japanese salutation, locale translation, dual-clock pairing user's local time with Tokyo's. The dual-clock stops feeling decorative the moment a fan checks before bed to see if their show has aired in Japan yet.

Fig. 04Welcome screen / metadata spec. Authored greeting matrix and dual-clock rule

Move 02 / A four-bucket module taxonomy

Editorial, behavioral, algorithmic, time-bounded. Each type carries its own iconography, copy patterns, and rules for when content engineers swap copy dynamically versus when humans write the entries. Gave editorial and engineering a shared vocabulary, which is the move that unlocks operational velocity.

Move 03 / Hime as a search character with personality

Research showed fans had a parasocial relationship with Hime no other anime brand could replicate. I encouraged the manga-illustrated direction precisely because it gave Hime a coherent place to live, and argued for putting her at the AI search interface so the surface had a familiar voice. The aesthetic decision and the AI integration decision had to defend each other.

◈ Scope clarification / Ask Hime AI

My contribution here is the content direction: parasocial research finding, search-character framing, cross-discipline aesthetic-plus-AI argument. The separate speech dataset and ML training data architecture work (which moved voice search accuracy by ~30%) is documented separately and is not double-counted in this case study's results.

● Decision log / Vision review, Q3 2022 3 strategic bets
The bet
Manga-forward visual direction with Japanese cultural threading at the system level.
Why I argued for it
A neutral aesthetic concedes the cultural moat. Cultural specificity is what scales when the taxonomy lets every regional surface populate within the same system.
Pushback
Will it scale? Will it read as exclusionary in markets where the audience is broader than core fandom?
My answer
Vague aesthetics force per-market reinvention. Specific aesthetics with a strong taxonomy do the opposite.
Outcome
Direction approved. Taxonomy artifact became part of how the team defended the bet inside subsequent leadership conversations.
The system

A taxonomy. Four module families. The rules that held them together.

Vision pitches buy permission. Systems are what ship. The bulk of the work was building the content layer that let the new aesthetic do real operational work across 25+ countries.

Entry flow: cultural identity before the home loads.

Login, profile picker, and welcome run before any content surface appears, which makes them the most important content design real estate in the experience. They are also what gets cut first under deadline pressure. I argued they had to ship in lockstep with the home.

The contested rule: Tokyo time anchors the greeting

Engineering wanted the device's local clock because implementation was straightforward. UX Research had documented fans translating to JST every time they discussed simulcasts. Shipped behavior anchors the salutation to Tokyo time; device's local clock still appears in the corner. The salutation belongs to Japan. The timekeeping belongs to the fan.

The taxonomy that made specificity scalable.

The artifact I'm proudest of. Every rail on the previous surface shipped with the same visual treatment, the same copy pattern, no shared rules. I authored a four-bucket taxonomy that gave every module its own identity, iconography, copy rules, and a defensible answer to: when can the system swap copy automatically versus when does an editor have to write it?

What the taxonomy unblocked

Content Engagement adopted the four buckets as their internal collection-logic spec. Editorial knew which rails they owned. Engineering knew which copy fields were dynamic. Localization knew which strings needed translation versus which surfaced from user state. One artifact, four teams unblocked.

Editorial in practice: Featured 10 earns its number.

Vertical poster art. Hand-drawn 1, 2, 3 numbered ranking. I held the line that the rail never falls back to algorithmic fill, which would defeat the editorial declaration. When editorial cannot fill it, the rail does not appear at all.

The metadata rule: restraint while video plays.

Required fields: Tag, Rating + Fan Score + Genre, Description. The rule that took three review cycles to land: no description copy while the video preview is playing. Description on top of moving anime art competes with the thing it is selling.

Fig. 13Module metadata / spec. Restraint as a rule

The line I defended hardest.

Premium Only sells the subscription against the most visual frame on the surface. Four lines, in order: Unlock the whole library (scope). No ads (quality). New episodes shortly after Japan (the line I defended hardest). Cancel anytime (permission to try). Marketing wanted "Same-day simulcast." Legal wanted "Episodes available within hours of Japanese broadcast." Both technically accurate, both emotionally dead. Shortly after Japan speaks to a fan following the manga, refreshing forums, worried about spoilers.

The measurement framework

Before this work, every rail rolled up to one engagement metric. The taxonomy unblocked a more honest model. Editorial measures on lift (curated entry vs algorithmic baseline, matched cohort). Behavioral measures on re-engagement. Algorithmic measures on banded CTR with a confidence-tier baseline. Time-bounded measures on attendance and post-event retention. Before this, the team could not answer "is editorial outperforming the recommender?" After, they could.

Reflection

What this taught me about staff-level content design.

◈ Reflection / 01 The most important decision was not a copy decision

The single most consequential move was arguing that content systems thinking belonged at the executive pitch from day one. When I encouraged the manga direction with the Senior Principal Designer, I was choosing a content surface that could carry Japanese cultural moments without feeling like cosplay. Content design at staff level is the architecture under the visual choice, and it has to be present when the visual choice gets approved.

◈ Reflection / 02 The pushback I expected versus the pushback I got

I went in prepared to defend the Japanese-forward direction itself. The pushback was about scale: would it hold up across 25+ markets without becoming exoticized in some and underwhelming in others? The framework gave the brand permission to be specific because the architecture made specificity scalable.

◈ Reflection / 03 What this prepared me to do next

The artifacts in this case study are the kit I bring into a staff-level content systems role. The taxonomy generalizes to any catalog product. The measurement rubric to any rail-based surface. The workshop methodology to anywhere brand voice and product copy diverge. The localization framework to any product staying culturally specific while scaling across markets.

First ninety days on a new team: audit the existing taxonomy or author one, establish a measurement partnership with data so each module family has a defensible axis, ship one shared documentation artifact across three teams in the first quarter. That is what hiring a staff content designer should buy a team. The proof is the chapter you just read.
